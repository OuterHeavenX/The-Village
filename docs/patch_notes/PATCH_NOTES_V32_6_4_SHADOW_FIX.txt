THE VILLAGE — V32.6.4
ROOF FLASHING FIXED

THE PROBLEM
Roofs and walls flashed with crawling stripes whenever you walked. That was
shadow acne, and V32.6.4 fixes it. It was introduced in V32.6.2.

To save memory on iPad, V32.6.2 halved the shadow map from 2048 to 1024 but left
the bias values that were tuned for the larger map. Halving the map doubles the
world size of a shadow texel, so the offset that stops a surface from shadowing
itself ended up about 4x too small. Every surface facing the moon shadowed itself
in stripes — and because the camera moves while the shadow map does not, those
stripes crawled across the roofs as you walked.

THE FIX
- Shadow bias is now calculated from the actual texel size instead of being
  hard-coded, so changing the shadow resolution can never break it again.
- The shadow camera's depth range was left at Three.js's default of 0.5 to 500.
  The scene only needs 1 to 140, so about 3.6x of the shadow map's depth
  resolution was being thrown away. Now tightened.
- The main camera ran near=0.1 against far=300, a 3000:1 ratio that wastes almost
  all depth precision. The camera never gets closer than about 15 units to
  anything, so near is now 1 — roughly 10x more depth precision, which also rules
  out genuine z-fighting on 16-bit mobile depth buffers.

FASTER TOO
The whole village was being re-rendered into the shadow map 60 times a second,
even though nothing that casts a shadow ever moves. Buildings, terrain and trees
are static, and the player is a sprite, which never casts. The shadow map is now
drawn once and redrawn only when you construct something or when Safari restores
a lost graphics context.

Measured: 0 shadow passes across 180 frames, down from 180.

One small trade: the ambient tree sway is 0.7 degrees, and it no longer animates
in the shadows. At this camera distance you will not see it.
